These Terms of Use govern the GRAM and LUNC swap service in Frontier Wallet. They set out the operation of the service, the commitments of buyers and sellers, applicable fees, and the support and compensation policy. An offer reservation and a smart contract lock are separate: releasing a reservation does not release funds locked on a blockchain.

1. About the service

The service enables users to exchange GRAM on the TON network for LUNC on Terra Classic directly with one another. It is operated by the Delfos Hub team ("Delfos Hub", "we", "us" or "our"). By using the service, you accept these Terms of Use. In these terms, the buyer provides GRAM to acquire LUNC, and the seller provides LUNC to receive GRAM.

Wallet control. Your wallet keys remain on your device, encrypted with your PIN. Delfos Hub does not take custody of your funds and cannot transfer them at its discretion.

Smart contract custody. During a swap, funds are held in hash time locked contracts (HTLCs). Each contract specifies an expiry time and the addresses entitled to receive claims or refunds. The contracts are immutable: neither Delfos Hub nor any other party can change their terms or redirect their payouts.

Native assets. The service does not create wrapped tokens or bridge assets between networks. GRAM remains on TON and LUNC remains on Terra Classic.

Transactions between users. Sellers publish offers and buyers accept all or part of them. Delfos Hub is not a party to these trades and does not determine their prices.

2. How a swap works

The buyer locks funds first. The seller locks funds only after verifying the buyer’s lock on the blockchain. A swap proceeds as follows:

  1. The seller publishes a signed offer specifying the amount of LUNC, the price in GRAM and the minimum amount a buyer may accept.

  2. The buyer accepts an amount from the offer. The buyer’s wallet generates and stores a secret on the device, then locks the required GRAM, service fee and network reserve in a TON smart contract. A claim requires the corresponding secret.

  3. The seller verifies the buyer’s lock and responds by locking the required LUNC, together with the Terra Classic burn tax, in a contract linked to the same secret.

  4. The relayer or the buyer’s wallet claims the LUNC for the buyer using the secret. This claim publishes the secret on Terra Classic.

  5. The relayer or the seller’s wallet uses the published secret to release the GRAM to the seller’s designated TON address.

Publishing the secret through a claim on one network enables the corresponding claim on the other. Funds that remain unclaimed may be refunded after the relevant lock expires, as described in section 7. The relayer submits claims and refunds through the contracts’ publicly callable functions; the dependencies associated with its operation are described in section 6.

3. Time limits and unanswered swaps

Each lock has an expiry time fixed at creation and enforced by its blockchain. The seller’s response window begins when the offers service records the buyer’s acceptance after verifying the GRAM lock.

Action or lock Time limit What happens at expiry
Seller response 30 minutes An unanswered acceptance counts as a miss. Its reservation is released at the next scheduled check, every 5 minutes.
Seller’s LUNC lock Up to 6 hours Unclaimed LUNC can be refunded only to the seller.
Buyer’s GRAM lock About 30 hours Unclaimed GRAM can be refunded only to the buyer.

If the seller does not respond, the buyer’s GRAM remains locked until the GRAM lock expires, approximately 30 hours after payment. Expiry of the 30 minute response window does not permit an early refund. This prevents a buyer from recovering the GRAM while still being able to claim LUNC locked by the seller.

Late responses. Frontier disables the Answer button after the 30 minute response window. The offers service may nevertheless accept a response submitted through another route if the offer has sufficient remaining capacity. The buyer’s wallet does not process that late response through its normal claim flow. Acceptance by the offers service therefore does not establish that the swap can complete in Frontier. Each party may recover any unclaimed funds after its own lock expires.

The seller’s LUNC lock expires at least 24 hours before the buyer’s GRAM lock, providing a claim window after the secret becomes public. Each wallet checks the other party’s lock before proceeding.

4. Offers, reservations and seller commitments

Offer duration. An offer remains available for no more than 24 hours and then expires automatically. A seller wishing to continue selling must publish a new offer.

Cancellation and performance. An offer cannot be edited. The seller may cancel it to prevent further acceptances, but cancellation does not remove the obligation to fulfil valid acceptances already made. Once a buyer has locked funds for a valid acceptance, the seller may not refuse that transaction. Publishing an offer constitutes a commitment to sell on its stated terms; the service provides no refusal function.

Fixed transaction terms. The price and amounts are determined by the seller’s signed offer. The buyer’s lock commits to that offer and the accepted amount.

When a reservation starts

A reservation is recorded only after the offers service verifies the buyer’s GRAM lock on TON. Selecting an amount or expressing an intention to buy does not reserve any part of an offer. The reserved amount is deducted immediately from the available balance. For example, accepting 30,000 LUNC from an offer of 100,000 LUNC leaves 70,000 LUNC available to other buyers.

The capacity check and reservation are performed in a single database operation to prevent simultaneous buyers from reserving the same amount. Reservations are stored off chain in the offers service’s Cloudflare database and persist through service restarts. The funds themselves remain in the immutable smart contracts.

Reservation release and sold amounts

The seller has 30 minutes to respond. If an acceptance remains unanswered, its reservation is released at the next scheduled check, which runs every 5 minutes. The amount then becomes available in the offer again. This release does not unlock the buyer’s GRAM; a refund remains subject to expiry of the GRAM lock.

An acceptance to which the seller has responded is recorded as sold by the offers service, even if the swap later fails and the seller recovers the LUNC. That amount is not automatically restored to the offer’s available balance.

Balances, limits and unanswered buys

Seller balance. An offer is accepted only if the seller’s balance covers that offer together with the seller’s other offers and pending acceptances. The seller must also maintain sufficient additional LUNC for the burn tax and network gas.

Abuse prevention. The offers service permits no more than 3 waiting acceptances per address and 20 announcements per minute per IP address. Each acceptance requires an actual GRAM lock lasting approximately 30 hours. Buyers must check their pending transactions before authorising any further payment.

Unanswered acceptances. An unanswered acceptance counts as a miss. A seller’s offers are hidden when, over the preceding 30 days, the seller has at least 3 misses and the number of misses exceeds one fifth of the number of responses. Offers become visible again automatically when these conditions cease to apply, whether through additional responses or expiry of older misses from the 30 day period. Sellers may enable Telegram alerts in the wallet to help them respond on time.

Buyer commitment. Accepting an offer commits the buyer’s GRAM on chain before the seller is notified. Funds are released through a successful swap or, if unclaimed, through a refund after expiry.

6. The relayer

Delfos Hub operates a relayer that submits the transactions needed to complete swaps on behalf of both parties. During normal relayer operation, users do not need to remain online or hold the other network’s native coin to pay claim gas. The fallback procedures and dependencies below continue to apply.

Functions. The relayer uses the secret supplied by the buyer’s wallet to claim LUNC for the buyer, then releases GRAM to the seller. It also submits refunds after locks expire.

Payout restrictions. The relayer cannot redirect contract payouts. Claimed LUNC is paid only to the buyer, claimed GRAM only to the seller, and refunds only to the party that originally locked the funds. These restrictions do not eliminate the dependencies associated with secret handling and transaction timing.

Timing and availability. The relayer is intended to publish the secret only through a claim of the buyer’s LUNC while the seller can still claim the GRAM. If the relayer becomes unavailable before the secret has been disclosed, unclaimed funds remain refundable after expiry. If the secret is already public, the seller’s wallet can claim the GRAM before the GRAM lock expires.

Secret handling. A buyer who supplies the secret to the relayer relies on it not to disclose that secret without claiming the LUNC. As a fallback, the buyer’s wallet attempts to claim the LUNC itself if the relayer has not done so in time, provided the wallet remains open.

Configured endpoint. Frontier communicates only with the relayer endpoint built into the application.

7. Refunds and recovery

If a swap does not complete, funds that remain unclaimed may be refunded after the relevant lock expires. When available, the relayer submits refunds automatically. Users may also initiate a refund in Frontier:

  • Buyer: select "Get my GRAM back" after the GRAM lock expires. The lock pays its own network fee; no separate payment is required.

  • Seller: select "Refund" after the LUNC lock expires. The transaction requires a small amount of LUNC for network gas.

Anyone may submit a refund transaction, but the contract pays only the original depositor. Refunds therefore remain possible if Frontier or the relayer is unavailable. The relevant contract can be identified using the buyer’s lock address or the seller’s lock number recorded in the transaction history.

Local wallet data. The buyer’s wallet stores the swap secret on the device that created it and supplies it to the relayer when using that service. The wallet recovery phrase does not restore the locally stored swap secret. Loss of that local data may prevent completion, but any GRAM that remains unclaimed can be refunded after the lock expires.
