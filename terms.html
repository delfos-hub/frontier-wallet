1. About the service

The GRAM ⇄ LUNC swap in Frontier Wallet lets two people exchange GRAM on the TON network for LUNC on Terra Classic directly with each other. It is run by the Delfos Hub team, [TBD: legal name and address] ("Delfos Hub", "we"). By using the swap you accept these terms.

  • Your keys stay with you. Your wallet's keys are kept on your device, encrypted with your PIN. We never hold your coins and cannot move them.
  • Smart contracts hold the coins during a swap. Each side's coins sit in a smart contract with a time limit. Every claim and refund pays only the addresses written into that contract when it was made. Nobody, including us, can change those contracts or redirect what they hold.
  • No bridge. No wrapped tokens are created: GRAM stays on TON, LUNC stays on Terra Classic.
  • Between users. Sellers publish offers and buyers take them. We are not a party to any trade and do not set prices.

2. How a swap works

The buyer always locks first; the seller locks only after checking the buyer's lock on chain.

  1. A seller publishes a signed offer: how much LUNC, the price in GRAM, and the smallest part a buyer may take.
  2. A buyer takes part of it. The buyer's wallet creates a secret, keeps it on the device, and locks the GRAM (with the fee and a small TON network reserve) in a TON contract that only that secret can open.
  3. The seller checks that lock and answers by locking the LUNC (with the Terra Classic burn tax) in a Terra Classic contract tied to the same secret.
  4. The buyer's LUNC is claimed with the secret, by the relayer (section 6) or by the buyer's wallet. This publishes the secret on Terra Classic.
  5. With the now public secret, the GRAM is released to the seller's TON address, by the relayer or by the seller's wallet.

A claim on one side publishes the secret that opens the other side. If a swap does not complete, each side gets its own coins back after its lock expires (section 7). The relayer only sends claims and refunds that anyone could send; how far you rely on it is explained in section 6.

3. Time limits and unanswered deals

Each lock has a fixed end time written into it when it is made, checked by its blockchain. The answer window is counted by our offers service from the moment it records the buyer's take.

What Time limit When it runs out
Seller answers a take 30 min The take is cancelled and counts as a miss for the seller
Seller's LUNC lock Up to 6 h The LUNC can be refunded, and only to the seller
Buyer's GRAM lock About 30 h The GRAM can be refunded, and only to the buyer

If the seller does not answer, the buyer's GRAM stays locked until its lock expires, about 30 hours after the buyer paid. The 30-minute window does not release it earlier: the contract allows no early refund, so that a buyer can never take the GRAM back and still receive LUNC a seller locked.

Frontier does not let a seller answer after the 30 minutes. A late answer sent some other way can still be recorded while the offer has room, but it never puts anyone's coins at risk: if the swap does not complete, each side gets its coins back when its lock expires.

The lock times are chosen so that, once the secret is public, the other side always has time to claim: the seller's LUNC lock ends at least 24 hours before the buyer's GRAM lock, and each wallet checks the other side's lock before acting.

4. Offers and sellers

  • Lifetime. An offer lasts at most 24 hours and then expires by itself. To keep selling, publish a new one.
  • Changes. An offer cannot be edited. A seller can cancel it at any time and publish a new one. Cancelling stops new takes; takes already made still need an answer.
  • Fixed terms for the buyer. The price and amounts come from the offer the seller signed, and the buyer's lock commits to that offer and amount.
  • No double sale. A take reserves its amount, so two buyers can never take the same LUNC. The amount returns to the offer if the seller does not answer in time. Once the seller has answered, it counts as sold, even if the swap later does not complete and the LUNC goes back to the seller.
  • Enough LUNC. An offer is accepted only if the seller's balance covers it together with the seller's other offers and pending takes. Keep extra LUNC for the burn tax and gas.
  • Misses. An unanswered take counts as a miss. A seller's offers are hidden from buyers when, over the last 30 days, the seller has at least 3 misses and they are more than a fifth of all answers. They return by themselves as old misses pass the 30 days or answers add up. Telegram alerts (in the wallet) help sellers answer in time.
  • Taking an offer is a real commitment for the buyer too: the GRAM is locked on chain before the seller is asked, and comes back only through the swap or after the lock expires.

6. The relayer

The relayer is a service run by Delfos Hub that finishes swaps for both sides, so neither side needs the other network's coin for gas or has to stay online.

  • What it does. It claims the LUNC for the buyer, with the secret the buyer's wallet gives it, then releases the GRAM to the seller. It also sends refunds when locks expire.
  • What it cannot do. Send coins anywhere else: the LUNC can only go to the buyer, the GRAM only to the seller, refunds only to whoever locked. If its server were broken into, the attacker could not take swap funds.
  • What you rely on it for. Timing. It publishes the buyer's secret only by claiming the LUNC for the buyer, and only while the seller's GRAM can still be released. If it goes down, nothing is lost: an unused secret stays private and both sides can refund, and a published secret lets the seller's wallet release the GRAM itself.
  • The one case it matters. A buyer who hands the secret to the relayer trusts it not to leak the secret without claiming. Frontier limits this: the buyer's wallet claims the LUNC itself if the relayer has not done so in time, while the wallet is open.

Frontier talks only to the relayer address built into the app.

7. Getting your coins back

If a swap does not complete, your coins come back after your lock expires. The relayer sends the refund by itself; you can also do it in Frontier:

  • Buyer: "Get my GRAM back" on the deal, after its lock expires. Free: the lock pays its own network fee.
  • Seller: "Refund" on the deal, after the LUNC lock expires. Costs a few LUNC of gas.

Refunds are open to anyone and always pay the person who locked, so they work even if Frontier or the relayer is unavailable, using the lock's address (buyer) or number (seller) from your transaction history.

Keep your wallet data. The swap's secret lives only on the device that made it; your recovery phrase does not restore it. If it is lost, the swap may not complete, but your GRAM still comes back when the lock expires.

8. Your responsibilities

  • Keys and PIN. Keep your recovery phrase private and safe. We cannot recover a lost phrase or PIN, and cannot reverse a transaction you signed.
  • Check before you sign. Read the confirmation screen: amounts, addresses, network. A transaction on chain is final.
  • Your TON address. As a seller, make sure the TON address that receives the GRAM is yours and correct. Avoid exchange deposit addresses, which may not credit a transfer from a contract.
  • Answer your takes. As a seller, keep enough LUNC for the lock, the burn tax and gas, and answer within 30 minutes.
  • Open your wallet when a deal waits. Frontier finishes your side by itself while it is open, if the relayer has not.
  • The law. Use the swap only where it is legal for you, and not if you are subject to sanctions. Taxes on your trades are your responsibility.
  • Age. You must be at least 18, or of legal age where you live.

9. Risks

Crypto swaps carry risks we cannot remove. By using the swap you accept them.

  • Smart contracts. The contracts were reviewed by the team in several audit rounds and have no owner or upgrade path, but code can still have flaws that lose funds.
  • Networks. TON or Terra Classic can halt, slow down or change their rules (fees, burn tax). A delay close to a time limit can make a swap fail.
  • Waiting. If a seller does not answer, your GRAM stays locked for about 30 hours (section 3).
  • Price. The price is fixed when you take an offer. The market may move while a swap is open.
  • The relayer. Swaps rely on it, or on your open wallet, to claim in time (section 6).
  • Your device. Malware, a lost phone or a shared PIN can expose your keys; a cleared wallet can lose an open swap's secret (section 7).
  • Scams. We will never ask for your recovery phrase, PIN or a swap secret. Use only the official Frontier Wallet bot.
